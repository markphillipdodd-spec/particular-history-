A.D. 325 · extended material

Council of Nicaea

Is the Son eternally and fully God, or a created being brought into existence by the Father?

Historical Context
Constantine, newly sole ruler of a reunified empire, convened roughly 300 bishops to end a dispute that threatened to fracture the church across his territory.
Controversy
Arius taught that the Son was the first and greatest of creatures — not eternal — such that "there was a time when he was not."
Confession / Clarification
The council confessed the Son as homoousios: of the same essence as the Father, not merely similar to him and not a creature at all.
Downstream Influence
Every later Christological council assumes this as settled. Reformation and Baptist confessions alike simply restate Nicene Trinitarianism as inherited ground, not open question.
